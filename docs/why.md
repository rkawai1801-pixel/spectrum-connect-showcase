# Why Spectrum Connect exists

## The short version

Parents of autistic children have to make big choices about treatments, often with no science
training and no time. The research that could help them exists, but it is hard to find, hard to
read, and often not trustworthy. Spectrum Connect reads that research the careful way, then explains
it in plain words, with proof behind every sentence.

## The problem, in four parts

### 1. Parents are forced to become researchers

When a child is diagnosed with autism, families get advice from everywhere: doctors, teachers,
other parents, social media, and companies selling products. Gaps in the medical, school and social
support systems mean parents often have to sort it out themselves. They end up asking the same
question over and over: **"Does this actually work, and is it safe?"**

### 2. The real answer is buried

The honest answer is usually spread across dozens or hundreds of research papers. Many are behind
paywalls. They are written for scientists. They often disagree. A careful review of all of them
normally takes a trained team many months.

### 3. Many published reviews skip the careful steps

There are well-known rules for doing a review properly (PRISMA, AMSTAR 2, GRADE and others). Studies
of published reviews show these rules are often skipped. For example, one study of 357 recent
treatment reviews found only **38% (135 of 357)** reported a registered plan before they started
(*Systematic Reviews*, 2022, 11:191). In some medical fields, most reviews checked with AMSTAR 2 are
rated "critically low" quality.

### 4. Nobody gives parents the careful version

Today's options split into three groups, and none does all of the job:

| | Careful (full methods) | Written for parents | Kept up to date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cochrane and other expert reviews | Yes | Hard to read | Often not |
| Parent websites and groups | Rarely | Yes | Varies |
| New AI research tools | Partly | No, built for researchers | Some |

## What Spectrum Connect does differently

1. **It does the careful version by default.** It follows the full set of rules that published
   reviews often skip: a locked plan, two readers, quality checklists, and a certainty rating.
2. **It writes for parents, and proves it to experts.** Every topic produces two documents: a short
   plain-language report for families, and a full audit document a scientist can check line by
   line.
3. **The AI does the reading. Rules and people make the calls.** This keeps the cost low enough to
   cover many questions, without asking anyone to "just trust the AI".
4. **It is honest about its limits.** Low certainty is reported as low. Missing pieces are named on
   the front page.

## Why it matters that this technology exists

- **Families make better choices with clear facts.** A parent who knows a treatment has "low
  certainty" evidence can ask better questions of their child's doctor.
- **It can protect children from harm.** Some treatments sold to families are useless, and some are
  harmful. Showing the evidence clearly, with sources, helps families avoid both.
- **It saves time for the people who help families.** Doctors, therapists and family support
  workers get a checked summary instead of starting from zero.
- **It shows AI can be used carefully in health.** The engine is built so every AI answer can be
  checked, every decision is made by a fixed rule or a named person, and nothing is hidden. That is
  a model for using AI where mistakes matter.
- **It can grow.** At a target cost of $1 to $3 of AI time per question, it can cover the hundreds
  of questions families actually ask, not just the few that big research groups have time for.
